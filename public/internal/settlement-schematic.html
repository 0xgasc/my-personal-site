Wallet opsec & settlement

Privacy boundary

Tracing forward from any payer reaches the shared treasury and stops. Public on-chain data past this point shows platform-level aggregate only, not per-merchant flows.

Two caveats: NOWPayments knows the mapping, so this is privacy from public observers and competitors — not from the intermediary or a regulator with legal process. And it holds only because the treasury is shared; per-merchant treasuries would rebuild the per-merchant revenue graph on-chain.

Chain strategies

The single-use-address-then-sweep model is not chain-neutral — each chain's fee mechanic reacts to it differently. The headline: Tron is the cheapest chain for reused addresses and one of the worst for single-use, because every fresh deposit address triggers a first-transfer energy penalty. Figures are order-of-magnitude at early-2026 prices and move with native-token price and congestion — re-check live before committing.

Address pool & replenishment

Each invoice gets a fresh deposit address. Those addresses come from a pool of pre-derived ones, ready to assign. When the pool runs low, the system generates more in a batch. Here's the full lifecycle.

When to generate a new seed (not just new addresses):
1. Every 10,000 derivations from one seed (precautionary limit — HD derivation is safe far beyond this, but smaller sets are easier to audit).
2. If a seed is suspected compromised — rotate immediately, sweep all funded addresses from that seed.
3. On the quarterly key rotation schedule (alongside cold wallet rotation).
4. When onboarding a new chain (one seed per chain, never shared across chains).

Expired addresses: If an invoice expires without payment, the address was never funded. On EVM chains it's free to recycle (just reassign it). On Solana, if the ATA was pre-created, the rent is stranded until you close it — run a reaper to close unfunded ATAs and reclaim rent. Don't reuse addresses even if they were never funded; single-use means single-use.

Treasury structure & key rotation

Two questions: "how much can I lose if a key gets stolen?" and "how long does one key matter?" The hot/cold split answers the first. Rotation answers the second. They work together but are configured independently.

Hot wallet $—

A small buffer that funds daily merchant payouts. Automated, single-sig or 2-of-3. If this key leaks, you lose only this amount — a few days of payouts, not your full balance.

Cold wallet $—

Everything else. Multisig (3-of-5), hardware keys, no automated spending. Money only moves out manually to refill the hot wallet. Rotation protects this one.

1 Create a brand-new cold wallet (fresh keys, fresh signers, done offline)
2 Move everything from old cold wallet → new cold wallet in one transaction
3 Record the swap in your books — same company, new address, date stamped
4 Deactivate old signers, confirm old wallet is empty, keep the address on file for audits

This is routine security, not money laundering. The difference: you rotate on a fixed schedule (every 90 days) and move the entire balance in one visible hop. A regulator following the trail sees a clean chain of custody handoffs — "same entity, new key, here's why, here's when." That's the opposite of splitting money across wallets to hide volume. Same tool, opposite intent — the trigger (time vs. volume) is what distinguishes them.

You are holding merchant funds. The moment you sweep deposits into a treasury you control, you're a custodian. In the US that means MSB registration (FinCEN). Elsewhere, the equivalent VASP/PSP license. This is solvable and legal — you apply, you register, you keep records. But on-chain data alone does not satisfy the recordkeeping requirements. You need your own books mapping wallets to merchants, transactions to invoices, and payouts to settlement dates.

Cross-chain treasury

With 3+ chains, you need a treasury strategy. Two models — pick based on where volume concentrates.

Per-chain treasuries Simpler

Each chain keeps its own hot + cold wallets. Sweeps stay on-chain. Payouts go out on the chain funds arrived on. No bridging risk.

Unified treasury (bridge) Capital efficient

Bridge all deposits to one treasury chain. Better capital efficiency, but adds bridge cost ($0.50–5/tx), bridge exploit risk, and finality latency.

Recommendation for launch: Start per-chain. Bridging adds a failure mode you don't need at low volume. Wait until one chain captures >70% of deposits, then consider making it the treasury chain. Cross-chain reconciliation is a database problem — your books already map each wallet to a chain, merchant, and invoice.
